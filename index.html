Hi! I'm a two-time Y Combinator founder. I'm currently the Co-Founder of Derya (YC S26), where we're building the operating layer for industrial sales and procurement: the messy, relationship-driven, spreadsheet-and-email machinery through which the physical world actually gets made and moved. Software ate the consumer; it hasn't yet touched the factory floor's back office. I think the next industrial revolution starts there.

Before Derya, I co-founded another YC company in freight and studied Computer Science at UCLA. I grew up in Turkey and competed for the Turkish national debating team, which is probably where I learned that most disagreements are really disagreements about definitions.

I read a lot of philosophy. Mark Fisher, Baudrillard, Foucault, and Derrida are the writers I keep returning to. Fisher's hauntology is the frame I can't shake: we live among the ghosts of futures that were promised and never arrived, and nowhere is that more literal than in heavy industry, where the tools of production are decades ahead of the tools used to buy and sell it. Building in this space feels like a small act of exorcism. I'm also interested in Deleuze's societies of control and what they mean for a world in which AI agents, rather than people, do most of the coordinating.

On the research side, I'm most curious about long-horizon agents that act on real-world state, where the hard problem is not intelligence but verification and trust, and about how markets behave when the negotiating parties on both sides are models. I think industrial procurement is one of the best places to study both.

I'd love to talk about any of the above, or about whatever you're building. You can reach me below.
