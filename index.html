Mert Turna

I'm the Co-Founder of Derya (YC S26). We're bringing forth the fifth industrial revolution by hyperconnecting industrial companies with agents. It's my second YC company; before that I studied Computer Science at UCLA.

I was born and raised in Istanbul, and I was building robots before I was doing much else; I picked it up at nine and never really put it down. Debate came at thirteen. I treated it the way I treated the robots, as a system to be understood and optimized, and that approach won me national and international championships over the years, most of them with the Turkish national team. Underneath, it was the fastest way I knew to take the world apart and see how its blood vessels ran. Building a company is how I get to do that at scale now.

Philosophy plays the same role for me. An engineer who only studies engines eventually stops seeing them; you have to look at the statues too. Mark Fisher's hauntology is the frame I can't shake: we live among the ghosts of futures that were promised and never arrived, and nowhere is that more literal than in heavy industry, where the machines are decades ahead of the paperwork used to buy and sell them. Deleuze's societies of control are the other one, especially what they become when agents, rather than people, do most of the coordinating.

That's also where my research interests sit: long-horizon agents acting on real-world state, where the hard problem is less intelligence than verification and trust, and what markets look like when both sides of a negotiation are models. The product I build all day turns out to be a good instrument for studying both.
